Two venues quote you similar figures. One turns out to be several thousand pounds more expensive. Nobody misled you — the quotes were simply written to different assumptions, and you compared the totals.

This is a method for taking any quote apart and putting all of them on the same footing.

## Start by finding the four numbers

Almost every quote is some combination of these, however it is laid out.

- **Hire fee** — the charge for the space and the time, whether or not anybody eats.
- **Per-head costs** — food, drinks packages, service. Multiply by your realistic guest number, not your optimistic one.
- **Minimum spend** — a floor, not a price. If your food and drink comes in under it, you pay the difference anyway.
- **Extras and surcharges** — corkage, overtime, cake cutting, chair covers, staff meals, cleaning, security.

Write the four numbers down for each venue. If a quote does not show one of them, that is the question to ask, not an absence of cost.

## Then normalise the quote

### Confirm whether VAT is included

A quote written without VAT is roughly a fifth cheaper than one written with it. Ask outright rather than assuming, and note the answer next to the figure.

### Fix the guest number

Reprice every quote at the same number of guests. Venues quote at different assumed numbers, and per-head pricing often steps at thresholds — the cost per person can change when you cross a band.

### Add the things you would buy anyway

If venue A includes linen, glassware and a cake stand and venue B does not, you are not comparing like with like until you have added the hire cost of those to B.

### Count the hours

Compare what time you get in and what time you must be out. An extra hour is usually purchasable, so price it and add it where you would want it.

## Read the small print for these five things

1. **Price uplifts.** Some contracts allow per-head prices to rise between booking and the event. Ask for the mechanism and, if you can, a cap.
2. **The deposit schedule.** When is each instalment due, and is any of it refundable?
3. **The cancellation ladder.** How much you lose usually increases as the date approaches. Note the dates.
4. **Final numbers deadline.** After this point, guests who drop out are still charged.
5. **Supplier restrictions.** An approved-supplier list changes what you will pay for photography, flowers and music, even though none of that appears on the venue quote.

## Build a single comparison line

For each venue, reduce everything above to one figure: *total cost for our guest count, VAT included, for the hours we want, including anything we would otherwise hire in.* That number is comparable. Nothing on the original quotes is.

Then keep a second, separate note of the things money does not capture — how the light falls in the afternoon, how far guests have to travel, whether the staff were straightforward with you. Those matter, but they should be a deliberate decision, not something hidden inside a price comparison.

## A note on deposits

A deposit is the point at which you stop shopping. Before you pay one, make sure you have the itemised quote in writing, the contract to read in full, and an answer on every extra you can foresee. It is entirely normal to ask for a few days to read a contract, and a venue that will not give you them has told you something useful.
