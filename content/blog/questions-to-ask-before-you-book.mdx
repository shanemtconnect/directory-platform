Most venue viewings run on charm. You are shown the best room in the best light, and an hour later you are being asked for a deposit. The questions below exist to get past that — they are the ones whose answers are hard to change once you have signed.

Take them to every viewing and ask the same questions at each. Comparing answers is far more useful than comparing photographs.

## Before you go

Ask these on the phone. If any answer rules the venue out, you have saved yourself a Saturday.

1. Is our date available, and how long will you hold it without a deposit?
2. What is the total cost for our guest numbers on that date, including everything you would normally add?
3. What is the minimum spend, if there is one, and what counts towards it?
4. Are you licensed for ceremonies, or would we need to marry elsewhere first?

## On the day of the viewing

### 1. What exactly does the price include?

Get this in writing, itemised. The gap between two quotes is usually not the venue — it is what each one assumes you will also buy. Ask specifically whether the figure covers staffing, tables and chairs, linen, glassware, setup and clearing, and whether **VAT is included or added**.

### 2. What are we required to buy from you?

Many venues require you to use their catering, their bar, or a list of approved suppliers. That is not unreasonable, but it removes your ability to shop around, so you need to know before you compare prices rather than after.

### 3. What is the corkage or supplier surcharge?

If you can bring in your own drink or your own caterer, there is often a fee for doing so. Ask for the figure, and ask whether it is per bottle, per head or a flat charge.

### 4. Who will actually be here on the day?

The person showing you round is often in sales. Ask who runs the event, whether you will meet them beforehand, and what happens if they leave the business between now and your date.

### 5. What is the wet-weather plan?

Every outdoor space has one. Ask to see it, not to hear about it. Stand in the room they would move you to and picture your full guest list in it.

### 6. What time does music have to stop, and why?

Licensing conditions and noise limiters are common, particularly at venues near housing. Ask whether there is a limiter, what level it is set to, and whether bands have had trouble with it.

### 7. How many events do you run in a day?

Two-a-day venues can be excellent, but you should know if you are the afternoon slot. Ask about turnaround time, shared access, and whether another party will be audible from your room.

### 8. What are the access times?

The difference between setup access from 9am and from 2pm changes what is possible for your suppliers, and sometimes what they charge.

### 9. What happens if numbers drop?

Ask when your final numbers are locked, how far you can move down from your original estimate, and what you pay for guests who do not come.

## The contract questions

### 10. What is the cancellation and postponement policy?

Read the actual clause. Note the dates at which the amount you lose increases, and whether a postponement is treated as a cancellation.

### 11. Can the price change after we sign?

Some contracts allow annual uplifts on per-head prices between booking and the event. If yours does, ask for a cap in writing.

### 12. What insurance do you hold, and what do we need?

Ask for evidence of their public liability cover, and ask what they require from your suppliers. It is a dull question that occasionally saves a lot of money.

## Answers that should give you pause

- *"Everyone asks that, don't worry about it."* A reasonable venue answers reasonable questions.
- A verbal figure that is never confirmed in writing.
- Pressure to decide today because the date will go otherwise. Dates do go — but a venue that is good will still be good tomorrow.
- Reluctance to put you in touch with anyone who has held an event there.

## Write the answers down

Take notes at each viewing on the same sheet, in the same order. A week later the buildings blur together and the notes are what you will actually decide on.
